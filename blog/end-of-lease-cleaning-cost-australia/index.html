Short answer

Typical end of lease cleaning prices by property size



  • Studio / 1 bedroom unit: $250 - $450

  • 2 bedroom unit or townhouse: $350 - $650

  • 3 bedroom house: $450 - $850

  • 4+ bedroom house: $600 - $1,200+


These are indicative market ranges gathered from published Australian cleaning rates, not fixed quotes. Every property is priced on its own condition.



Does the price change between cities?


Yes, but less than people expect. Sydney and Melbourne sit at the higher end of the ranges above, Brisbane, Perth, Adelaide and Canberra sit mid range, and regional areas such as Wollongong, Newcastle and the Illawarra are usually slightly below capital city pricing. Labour cost, travel distance and demand at the end of the month are the main drivers.



What actually drives the price up



  • Condition, not size. A neglected two bedroom unit can cost more than a well maintained four bedroom house.

  • Ovens and rangehoods. Heavy baked-on grease can add an hour or more on its own.

  • Carpets. Most leases require professional steam cleaning. Budget roughly $35 - $60 per room on top of the clean.

  • Mould and soap scum. Bathrooms with long-standing mould take significantly longer.

  • Walls, blinds and windows. Spot-cleaning walls, washing venetian blinds and external window access all add time.

  • Garages, balconies and yards. Frequently excluded from base quotes, so always ask.



Hourly rate vs fixed price bond clean, which is cheaper?


Hourly is usually cheaper when the property has been kept in good condition, because you only pay for the time actually needed. Fixed price is safer when the property is in poor condition or you want absolute cost certainty before you commit.


A fair hourly rate for bond cleaning in Australia in 2026 sits around $55 - $75 per hour per cleaner. Anything far below that usually means corners get cut, and a failed final inspection costs far more than the saving.



Is end of lease cleaning worth paying for?


Financially, usually yes. A typical bond in Australia is four weeks rent, often $2,000 - $4,000. Agents commonly deduct $150 - $400 to send their own cleaner back for a failed inspection, and that deduction is almost always more than the difference between a cheap clean and a professional clean with a clearly agreed inspection scope.



Questions to ask before you book



  • Is the quote inclusive or exclusive of GST?

  • What is the agreed cleaning scope, and how are concerns reviewed after inspection?

  • Are carpets, ovens, windows, blinds and the garage included?

  • Are the cleaners insured and police checked?

  • What happens if the agent asks for a re-clean?

Frequently asked questions

How much does end of lease cleaning cost in Australia?

Most Australian renters pay about $250 to $450 for a one bedroom unit, $350 to $650 for a two bedroom, and $500 to $1,000 or more for a three to four bedroom house in 2026. Carpet steam cleaning is normally quoted separately. Condition matters more than size.

How much does an end of lease clean cost per hour?

A fair hourly rate for bond cleaning in Australia in 2026 is roughly $55 to $75 per hour per cleaner. Illawarra Cleaning Crew charges $71.50 per hour incl. GST with a six hour minimum, so from $429 incl. GST.

Is carpet cleaning included in an end of lease clean?

Usually not. Professional carpet steam cleaning is normally a separate line item, commonly $35 to $60 per room, because it needs different equipment. Most Australian leases require it if the property was carpeted at the start of the tenancy.

Do I have to pay for a professional bond clean?

No. In every Australian state you can clean the property yourself, as long as it is returned in the condition recorded on the entry condition report, fair wear and tear excepted. A landlord cannot legally require a professional cleaning receipt unless the tenancy agreement lawfully specifies it, which varies by state.

Is end of lease cleaning worth the cost?

Usually yes. Agents commonly deduct $150 to $400 from the bond to send their own cleaner back after a failed inspection, which is generally more than the cost difference between a cheap clean and a professional clean with a clearly agreed inspection scope.
